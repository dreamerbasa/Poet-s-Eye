# Haiku Spec

## What a haiku is

17 syllables. 5-7-5 structure. But that's the container, not the poem. What makes it a haiku and not just a short sentence broken into three lines:

### One moment of noticing
A haiku captures a single instant where something in the world snagged attention. Not a narrative, not a reflection, not a summary. A moment frozen mid-breath.

### Two parts with a cut between them
This is the core mechanic. Line 1 sets up one image. Lines 2-3 place a second image next to it (or line 1-2 set up, line 3 cuts). The poet never explains the connection. The gap between the two images is where the reader makes meaning. This is non-negotiable — without the cut, it's just a short observation.

### Concrete images only
Every word should point at something you could photograph, touch, hear, or smell. The moment you use an abstraction (beauty, time, love, hope), the haiku dies. The abstraction is what the reader arrives at after reading the concrete images. You don't serve them the conclusion.

### The small carries the large
A haiku about grief doesn't mention grief. It mentions the second toothbrush still in the holder. A haiku about chaos doesn't say chaos. It says the price tag still stuck to the bottom of the shoe at the wedding. Specific, small, physical — implying something much bigger.

## What a haiku is NOT

This list matters more than the one above because the model will default to every single one of these.

### Not a caption
"Beautiful temple stands / ancient wisdom in its walls / peace for all who come" — this is an Instagram caption with line breaks. Nothing was noticed. Nothing was juxtaposed.

### Not a summary of the image
The haiku doesn't describe what's in the image. It takes one thread from the image and pulls it somewhere unexpected.

### Not a greeting card
No forced positivity. No life lessons. No "and yet life goes on" endings. No "reminds me that..." or "teaches us to..." constructions. If the image is of a rainy parking lot, the haiku should feel like a rainy parking lot, not a motivational poster about finding beauty in storms.

### Not narration
"The old man sat down / he thought about his lost wife / tears fell on his hands" — this tells a story in miniature. A haiku doesn't narrate. It presents and leaves.

### Not a word puzzle
Don't contort syntax to hit 5-7-5. If the natural phrasing is 5-8-4, rework the idea, not just the words. Awkward phrasing to force syllable count kills the poem.

### Not precious about being poetry
No "o'er" or "upon" or "whilst." No archaic language. No trying to sound literary. The language should be plain, direct, modern.

## How the cut works

This is the hardest part.

**Bad — no cut, just continuous description:**
```
morning chai steams up
the kitchen window is warm
sunlight fills the room
```
This is one image stretched across three lines. No juxtaposition. Nothing for the reader to complete.

**Good — real cut:**
```
morning chai steam—
on the window, a handprint
too small to be hers
```
Line 1: chai, steam, morning domesticity. Cut. Lines 2-3: a child's handprint on the glass. The reader fills in the gap — whose child? where are they now? The poem opens a door instead of closing one.

---

## What the last line should do

Open a door, never close one. The last line is where bad haiku moralize and good haiku create space.

Test: if you can add "...and that's beautiful" after the last line without it feeling redundant, the last line already said "and that's beautiful" — which means it failed.

The last line should make the reader pause. Not because it's clever, but because it implies something they have to sit with.

---

## Banned words

Never use any of these in a haiku: beauty, beautiful, peace, peaceful, soul, heart, dream, hope, joy, love, life, nature, silence (the word — you can depict silence), eternity, forever, wisdom, serene, tranquil, gentle, graceful, harmony, bliss, divine, sacred, precious, tender, embrace, cherish, whisper (unless someone is literally whispering).
